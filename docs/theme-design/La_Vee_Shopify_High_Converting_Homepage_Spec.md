# La Vee — High-Converting Shopify Homepage Design Specification

**Document type:** Homepage UX/UI + Conversion Specification  
**Platform:** Shopify  
**Brand:** La Vee Design  
**Primary channel assumption:** Instagram / social-led fashion traffic  
**Recommended theme starting point:** Shopify Prestige (or equivalent premium fashion theme)  
**Version:** v1.0 — September 2026

---

## 1. Objective

The La Vee homepage should feel like a **premium women's fashion campaign**, but behave like a **high-performance ecommerce landing page**.

The design must achieve four things quickly:

1. Make the visitor understand the La Vee aesthetic within the first screen.
2. Help her find the right product/category with minimal thinking.
3. Build enough desire and trust to move from browsing to product consideration.
4. Reduce friction between Instagram → product → size selection → cart.

The homepage should not look like a generic Shopify template or a crowded marketplace. The desired balance is:

> **House of CB femininity + MESHKI merchandising + DISSH minimalism + Oh Polly social commerce + Reformation simplicity.**

---

# 2. R&D Findings from Reference Fashion Brands

## MESHKI — Key lesson: campaign-led commerce

What to learn:

- Large, aspirational campaign photography establishes the brand immediately.
- Newness and collections are highly visible.
- Product discovery begins quickly after brand storytelling.
- Commercial CTAs are clear without making the site feel discount-heavy.
- Product merchandising supports fast purchase decisions.

**Use for La Vee:** strong hero, New Arrivals, Best Sellers, collection-led merchandising, Quick Add where appropriate.

---

## House of CB — Key lesson: feminine premium positioning + fit-led discovery

House of CB combines luxury-style visual presentation with detailed shopping filters such as style, size, colour, cup size, length and fabric.

What to learn:

- A fashion brand can feel premium while still being extremely product-oriented.
- Occasion/style-based shopping reduces decision friction.
- New arrivals are given a predictable rhythm.
- Fit and size confidence matter greatly for women's fashion.

**Use for La Vee:** occasion/category navigation, size confidence, fit information, structured collection names, premium photography.

---

## DISSH — Key lesson: restraint creates perceived value

What to learn:

- Strong whitespace makes photography and products feel more premium.
- Neutral design lets garments become the visual focus.
- Typography is elegant but does not fight with product photography.
- Pages avoid unnecessary decorative UI.

**Use for La Vee:** clean layout, generous spacing, limited colors, sophisticated typography, minimal borders and visual noise.

---

## Oh Polly — Key lesson: social-commerce merchandising

Oh Polly uses Shop the Look, Quick Add, rewards/trust content and strong social-style product imagery.

What to learn:

- Visitors from Instagram respond well to complete looks, not only isolated SKUs.
- Shop the Look makes styling inspiration directly shoppable.
- Quick Add can shorten the path to cart.
- Trust/reassurance content can be visually integrated instead of placed only in the footer.

**Use for La Vee:** Shop the Look, Instagram/UGC content, Quick Add, reassurance strip.

---

## Reformation — Key lesson: simple navigation + editorial merchandising

What to learn:

- Strong editorial campaign content can coexist with clear product categories.
- Category entry points such as Dresses, Tops, Sets and occasion collections are easy to scan.
- Homepage sections can remain visually simple even with a large catalog.

**Use for La Vee:** direct category navigation, short copy, editorial/product alternation.

---

# 3. Core Homepage Strategy

The homepage should follow this psychological sequence:

**Attention → Identity → Discovery → Desire → Product → Proof → Styling Inspiration → Trust → Retention**

Translated into Shopify sections:

1. Announcement Bar
2. Header / Navigation
3. Hero Campaign
4. Shop by Category
5. New Arrivals
6. Editorial Campaign / Brand Moment
7. Best Sellers
8. Shop the Look
9. Occasion / Collection Discovery
10. Brand Value / Why La Vee
11. Reviews / Social Proof
12. Instagram / Community UGC
13. Email / Early Access
14. Footer

The homepage should generally contain **8–12 meaningful content sections before the footer**, but each section should be visually focused. Avoid adding sections simply because the theme provides them.

---

# 4. Visual Direction

## Overall mood

La Vee should feel:

- Feminine
- Modern
- Aspirational
- Confident
- Fashion-editorial
- Premium but approachable
- Social-first rather than corporate

Avoid:

- Heavy gradients
- Too many card borders
- Multiple bright accent colors
- Excessive icons
- Marketplace-style density
- Constant sale badges
- Too many popups
- Small unreadable fashion typography

---

## Color system

The website shell should remain mostly neutral so campaign imagery provides color.

Suggested structure:

- **Primary background:** warm ivory / soft white
- **Primary text:** charcoal / near black
- **Secondary background:** soft stone / nude / blush-neutral
- **Accent:** one La Vee signature color derived from the brand identity
- **Sale/error only:** muted red, used sparingly

Rule: **Do not introduce more than one strong brand accent color in the ecommerce UI.**

---

## Typography

Recommended pairing:

- **Editorial headings:** elegant serif or refined fashion display font
- **Navigation/body/product UI:** modern clean sans-serif

Usage:

- Hero: editorial, emotional
- Product title / price / buttons: simple sans-serif
- Avoid all-caps everywhere; reserve uppercase for small navigation labels or eyebrow text.

### Suggested typography scale

Desktop:

- Hero H1: 52–72 px
- Section H2: 32–44 px
- Editorial H2: 40–56 px
- Body: 15–17 px
- Product title: 14–16 px
- Utility/nav: 12–14 px

Mobile:

- Hero H1: 34–44 px
- Section H2: 26–32 px
- Body: 15–16 px
- Buttons: minimum 15 px text

# 5. Detailed Homepage Sections

## Section 01 — Announcement Bar

### Purpose

Communicate **one high-value reason to buy** before the customer begins browsing.

### Recommended content

Choose one primary message at a time:

- Free shipping threshold
- Easy returns/exchanges
- New collection launch
- Limited early access

Example:

> Complimentary shipping on orders over [threshold]

or

> The New Collection Has Arrived — Explore Now

### UX rules

- Height: approximately 28–36 px desktop, 30–40 px mobile.
- Do not rotate 4–5 messages rapidly.
- One message is preferable; maximum two rotating messages.
- Text should be clickable if it references a collection/promotion.

### Conversion reason

It answers a purchase objection before the visitor even reaches a product.

---

## Section 02 — Header / Navigation

### Purpose

Make product discovery effortless without damaging the luxury aesthetic.

### Desktop structure

**Left:** Shop navigation  
**Center:** La Vee logo  
**Right:** Search / Account / Wishlist optional / Cart

Suggested primary navigation:

- NEW IN
- DRESSES
- SETS
- TOPS
- BOTTOMS
- OCCASION
- BEST SELLERS
- SALE *(only when strategically needed)*

If La Vee's actual catalog differs, replace categories with its strongest commercial categories.

### Mega menu

Use image-supported mega menus for high-priority areas.

Example under **DRESSES**:

- Mini
- Midi
- Maxi
- Evening
- Party
- Wedding Guest
- Best Sellers

Plus one campaign image with CTA:

> The Dress Edit → Shop Now

### Mobile rules

- Hamburger / menu button
- Centered or left-aligned logo
- Search and cart immediately visible
- Menu opens full height
- Show New In, Best Sellers and Dresses near the top
- Avoid 3–4 nesting levels

### Sticky behavior

Use a sticky header after scrolling, but make it compact.

### Conversion reason

Fashion visitors frequently know **what type of item** they want before knowing the exact product.

---

## Section 03 — Hero Campaign

### Purpose

Create immediate desire and establish brand identity.

### Recommended format

A full-width image or controlled short video, ideally featuring a model wearing a hero product/collection.

### Content structure

Small eyebrow:

> NEW COLLECTION

Headline:

> Made to Be Remembered.

Supporting line:

> New silhouettes for evenings, celebrations and everything after.

Primary CTA:

> SHOP NEW IN

Secondary CTA:

> SHOP DRESSES

### Design rules

- One strong message.
- Maximum two CTAs.
- Avoid paragraph-length text.
- CTA must have sufficient contrast over photography.
- Do not make customers guess whether the image is clickable.

### Desktop dimensions

Target visual height: **70–90vh**, depending on photography.

### Mobile

Do not simply crop desktop assets.

Prepare a dedicated **4:5 or 9:16 mobile hero asset** with the model/product placed so text remains readable.

Hero should expose CTA without requiring a large scroll.

### Image/video rule

Photography quality matters more than animation. If video is used:

- muted
- short loop
- compressed
- no loading delay that hides CTA
- poster image supplied

### Conversion principle

**Sell the outcome/emotion first, then provide the shopping path immediately.**

---

## Section 04 — Shop by Category

### Purpose

Turn brand interest into browsing intent quickly.

### Recommended categories

Use 3–5 categories only.

Example:

1. Dresses
2. Sets
3. Tops
4. Occasion

### Layout

Desktop:

- 4 equal editorial image cards

Mobile:

- 2-column grid, or
- horizontal swipe cards if photography benefits from larger cards

Each card contains:

- strong lifestyle/product image
- category name
- subtle CTA such as “Shop Dresses”

### Avoid

Generic icon categories or tiny thumbnail circles if the brand wants a premium image.

### Conversion reason

A first-time visitor may not understand La Vee's catalog. Category tiles turn an open-ended decision into 3–5 easy choices.

---

## Section 05 — New Arrivals Product Carousel/Grid

### Purpose

Show actual products before the visitor gets too far down the homepage.

### Heading

> NEW ARRIVALS

Optional microcopy:

> Just landed.

CTA:

> View All New Arrivals

### Desktop

4 products visible per row.

### Mobile

Approximately 1.5–2.2 cards visible so the customer understands the row can swipe.

### Product card information

Show:

- Product image
- Secondary image on hover / swipe optional
- Product name
- Price
- Color swatches only when useful
- New / Best Seller badge only when meaningful
- Quick Add

### Quick Add behavior

When clicked:

- If no size selection is needed: add directly.
- If size is required: open a lightweight size picker / quick view.

Do **not** add a random default size.

### Photography

Use consistent crop/ratio, preferably 3:4 or 4:5.

Product cards should look like a fashion editorial grid, not marketplace tiles.

### Conversion reason

Social visitors should reach something purchasable within the first few homepage sections.

---

## Section 06 — Editorial Campaign / Brand Moment

### Purpose

Stop the homepage from becoming a catalog grid and increase perceived brand value.

### Layout options

**Option A:** 50/50 split — image + editorial text  
**Option B:** full-width campaign image with minimal overlay

Example:

> THE AFTER-DARK EDIT
>
> Sculpted silhouettes. Soft details. Designed for the night.
>
> Explore the Edit

### Rules

- This section should communicate one collection, story or style theme.
- Avoid explaining the entire company history here.
- Use campaign photography distinct from product-grid photography.

### Conversion reason

Premium fashion conversion depends partly on **desire and perceived value**, not only transactional UX.

---

## Section 07 — Best Sellers

### Purpose

Reduce uncertainty through implied popularity and help new visitors start with proven products.

### Heading

> MOST WANTED

or

> BEST SELLERS

### Product count

Show approximately 4–8 products.

### Card functionality

Same product card system as New Arrivals.

If genuine customer data exists, dynamically maintain this collection based on sales/merchandising rules.

### Important

Do not label products “Best Seller” unless there is a defensible internal merchandising reason.

### Conversion reason

Best sellers reduce choice anxiety for visitors unfamiliar with the brand.

---

## Section 08 — Shop the Look

### Purpose

Translate Instagram-style inspiration into a shoppable outfit.

This is one of the strongest sections for a social-first fashion brand.

### Recommended structure

Large lifestyle photo of a complete outfit.

Clickable hotspot or adjacent product list:

- Top
- Skirt/trouser
- Dress
- Outer layer
- Accessory, if sold

CTA:

> SHOP THE LOOK

### Alternative layout

Image on left; products on right.

Mobile:

Image → products directly beneath it.

### Conversion opportunity

Enable multiple complementary products to be added without repeatedly navigating away.

### Principle

**Style the customer, not just the SKU.**

---

## Section 09 — Shop by Occasion / Collection

### Purpose

Match products to the customer's real reason for shopping.

Fashion purchases are often mission-based rather than category-based.

Recommended tiles depend on catalog, for example:

- Date Night
- Wedding Guest
- Party
- Vacation
- Evening
- Everyday Elevated

Do not display six options if La Vee currently has only enough products to support three strong ones.

### Layout

2–3 large editorial tiles, asymmetrical layout if the theme permits.

### Conversion reason

“Wedding Guest” is often easier for a shopper to act on than deciding between technical product categories.

---

## Section 10 — Why La Vee / Brand Value Strip

### Purpose

Answer: **Why should I buy this here instead of another Instagram fashion brand?**

### Recommended content

Use 3 concise value propositions.

Examples only — replace with claims that are actually true:

- Designed for a flattering fit
- Thoughtful fabrics & finishing
- Easy exchanges

or brand-specific differentiators such as:

- Limited-edition drops
- Original La Vee designs
- Locally made / handcrafted

### Layout

Desktop: 3 columns  
Mobile: stacked or horizontally swipeable

Small icons may be used but should be refined, not playful ecommerce clip art.

### Important

Only make claims that La Vee can operationally support.

---

## Section 11 — Reviews / Social Proof

### Purpose

Reduce uncertainty and provide proof beyond brand-created marketing.

### Recommended version

Heading:

> WORN & LOVED

Show:

- star rating summary if enough verified reviews exist
- 3–5 strong customer reviews
- customer first name / verified buyer where permitted
- product purchased
- optional customer photo

### Best review content

Prioritize reviews mentioning:

- fit
- sizing
- fabric/quality
- compliments received
- occasion worn
- delivery/service

### Avoid

An endless generic carousel of “Love it!!!” reviews.

### Conversion reason

For apparel, social proof is most valuable when it reduces **fit and quality uncertainty**.

---

## Section 12 — Instagram / Community / UGC

### Purpose

Connect the ecommerce store back to the brand's strongest social channel.

### Heading ideas

> @LAVEEDESIGN

> Styled by You

> The La Vee Community

### Content

4–6 curated high-quality posts/reels/customer images.

Prioritize content where the clothing is clearly visible.

### Functionality

Where possible, make images **shoppable** rather than merely linking visitors away to Instagram.

### Important

Do not slow the homepage with a poorly optimized Instagram feed widget. Curated Shopify-managed content is preferable if the live widget hurts Core Web Vitals.

### Conversion reason

UGC helps Instagram visitors experience continuity between the social brand and the store.

---

## Section 13 — Email / Early Access

### Purpose

Capture value from visitors who like the brand but are not ready to buy today.

### Positioning

Do not use generic:

> Subscribe to our newsletter.

Use a fashion-specific benefit:

> FIRST ACCESS TO WHAT'S NEXT
>
> New drops, restocks and private offers — before everyone else.

Email field + CTA:

> JOIN LA VEE

### Incentive

A discount can be tested, but premium positioning should not depend on permanent 10–15% discount messaging.

Alternative incentive:

- Early access
- Restock priority
- Private edits
- Birthday offer

---

## Section 14 — Footer

### Recommended structure

### SHOP

- New In
- Dresses
- Sets
- Best Sellers
- Sale

### HELP

- Contact
- Shipping
- Returns & Exchanges
- Size Guide
- FAQ
- Track Order

### ABOUT

- Our Story
- Instagram
- Privacy
- Terms

### Utility

- Country/currency selector if needed
- Social icons
- Payment icons kept subtle
- Copyright

### Rule

The footer is the correct place for secondary information. Do not overload the main header with these links.

# 6. Product Card Specification

Every homepage product section should use the same reusable product card.

## Required elements

1. Primary product image
2. Hover/second image
3. Product title
4. Price
5. Color options/swatches if multiple
6. New / Best Seller / Low Stock badge only where accurate
7. Quick Add
8. Wishlist optional

## Interaction

Desktop:

- hover swaps to secondary image
- Quick Add becomes visible without obscuring product

Mobile:

- no hover dependency
- Quick Add permanently accessible but visually subtle

### Product image consistency

Use the same aspect ratio throughout homepage grids.

Recommended: **4:5** for a model-led women's fashion store.

---

# 7. Mobile-First Requirements

Shopify's 2026 ecommerce guidance emphasizes mobile usability; mobile should be treated as the primary design, particularly for an Instagram-led brand.

## Mandatory mobile behaviors

- Dedicated hero crop/assets
- Thumb-friendly CTAs
- Minimum comfortable touch targets
- Main shopping categories visible early
- Search easy to access
- Sticky compact header
- Cart always understandable
- Product carousels swipe naturally
- No tiny text overlays on images
- Avoid intrusive full-screen popups immediately after arrival
- Lazy-load below-the-fold media

## Mobile homepage priority

The first mobile journey should roughly be:

**Hero → category → products**

Do not make users scroll through a long About/brand story before seeing items they can buy.

Recommended capabilities based on premium themes such as Prestige:

- Quick Buy / Quick Add
- Slide-out cart
- Sticky cart / sticky Add to Cart on PDP
- Size charts
- Color swatches
- Product video
- Product recommendations
- Recently viewed
- Cross-selling
- Lookbooks / image hotspots

- Search suggestions
- Promo tiles/banners

# 9. Homepage Content Hierarchy

Use this priority order:

### Level 1 — Brand + buying path

Hero / New In / key categories

### Level 2 — Commercial proof

New Arrivals / Best Sellers

### Level 3 — Desire

Campaigns / Shop the Look / Occasions

### Level 4 — Trust

Brand values / reviews / delivery-return reassurance

### Level 5 — Retention

Instagram / email / footer

This hierarchy prevents the homepage from becoming either:

- a beautiful lookbook that does not sell, or
- a crowded product feed that damages the premium brand.

---

# 10. Cart Drawer Recommendations

The homepage conversion flow continues into the cart drawer.

Recommended cart drawer contents:

1. Product image, title, size, color
2. Quantity control
3. Remove/edit
4. Shipping threshold progress message
5. 1–2 relevant cross-sells maximum
6. Total
7. Strong **CHECKOUT** CTA
8. Express payment option if appropriate
9. Small returns/shipping reassurance

Avoid aggressive popups after Add to Cart.

---

# 11. Promotion Strategy

For premium fashion positioning, promotions should feel intentional.

Use:

- launch events
- seasonal edits
- limited-time private sale
- early-access offers
- free shipping threshold

Avoid:

- constant countdown timers
- three simultaneous promotional bars
- permanent sitewide discount language
- fake scarcity
- excessive “ONLY 2 LEFT!!!” messaging unless inventory is genuinely low

**Premium perception is itself a conversion asset.**

---

# 12. Image & Creative Requirements

The site quality will depend heavily on photography.

## Every important collection should ideally have

- 1 hero/editorial image
- 1 vertical mobile crop
- clean product/model images
- detail shots
- back/side views
- optional short product video

## Homepage creative ratios

- Hero desktop: ~16:9 or wider campaign composition
- Hero mobile: 4:5 / 9:16
- Category cards: 4:5
- Product cards: 4:5
- Editorial panels: 4:5 or 3:4
- Instagram/UGC: naturally mixed but visually curated

## Photography rule

Use visual consistency in:

- lighting
- background
- editing
- model direction
- crop
- skin tone rendering

A premium Shopify theme cannot compensate for inconsistent creative assets.

---

# 13. Page Speed Guardrails

A fashion site can easily become slow because of large imagery and video.

## Rules

- Serve WebP/AVIF where Shopify supports it.
- Avoid unnecessarily huge source images.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold imagery.
- Prioritize hero asset loading.
- Use one hero video maximum.
- Avoid autoplay video sections repeated throughout homepage.
- Keep third-party Shopify apps limited.
- Avoid Instagram widgets that inject heavy JavaScript if a curated gallery can achieve the same goal.

Before launch, test mobile performance on real devices, not only desktop preview.

---

# 14. Popup Strategy

Do not show an email popup instantly.

Recommended triggers to test:

- after 8–15 seconds
- after 30–40% scroll
- exit intent on desktop
- second page view

Mobile popup should not cover the entire interface unnecessarily.

Offer:

> Join La Vee for first access to new drops and restocks.

Test discount vs non-discount acquisition separately.

---

# 15. Trust & Risk Reduction

Fashion shoppers often hesitate because of:

- size uncertainty
- fit uncertainty
- product quality
- how fabric looks in real life
- shipping time
- return complexity

Homepage and PDP must collectively address these.

## Homepage

- reviews
- UGC
- delivery/return reassurance
- brand credibility

## Product page

- size chart
- model measurements
- fit description
- fabric/composition
- detail imagery
- delivery estimate
- return policy summary
- reviews by size/fit where possible

Do not push all reassurance into the FAQ page.

# 16. Recommended Shopify Theme

## Primary recommendation: Prestige

Why it fits La Vee:

- positioned for premium/high-end brands
- strong editorial visual system
- 30+ configurable sections
- Quick Buy
- slide-out and sticky cart
- lookbooks
- size charts
- product video
- color swatches
- image hotspots
- recently viewed products
- recommended products
- mega menu
- product filtering
- promo sections
- cross-selling

The theme should be treated as a **framework**, not a finished design. Use La Vee's own typography, imagery, spacing, section sequence and art direction.

## Alternative directions

- **Broadcast:** stronger social/mobile commerce orientation
- **Impulse:** more promotion-driven / commercial
- **Symmetry:** useful when catalog size grows substantially

# 20. Tracking & Analytics Requirements

Do not judge the homepage only by how it looks.

Track at minimum:

- Homepage → collection click-through rate
- Homepage → PDP click-through rate
- Hero CTA click rate
- Category tile click rate
- Product-card Quick Add usage
- Homepage add-to-cart rate
- Product view → add-to-cart rate
- Cart → checkout rate
- Checkout → purchase rate
- Mobile vs desktop conversion
- New vs returning user conversion
- Instagram/social traffic conversion
- Email signup rate

Use Shopify Analytics plus GA4 where appropriate.

For behavior diagnostics, a privacy-compliant heatmap/session tool can help identify where people stop scrolling or fail to interact.

Do not run all tests at once.

# 23. What Not to Copy from Competitors

Reference brands should provide patterns, not a visual clone.

Do not copy:

- exact typography combinations
- photography concepts shot-for-shot
- branded terminology
- unique campaign copy
- layouts so literally that La Vee loses identity

Instead copy the **principles**:

- strong visual hierarchy
- obvious buying path
- category clarity
- fit confidence
- editorial desire
- social proof
- low-friction purchase flow

# 24. Final Recommended La Vee Homepage Formula

If only one version is built first, use this:

> **Announcement → Minimal Header → Campaign Hero → 4 Categories → New Arrivals → Editorial Story → Best Sellers → Shop the Look → Occasion Edit → 3 Brand Benefits → Reviews → Shoppable Instagram/UGC → Early Access → Footer**

This creates the desired balance:

**Brand desirability + fast shopping + proof + low friction.**

The homepage should feel luxurious enough that customers want the clothes, while remaining simple enough that buying never becomes work.
